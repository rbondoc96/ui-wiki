"Where does navigation live" is a decision you make on every new app, and the
three answers are nearly always the same: a **bottom tab bar**, a **sidebar**, or
a **top nav**. The right one falls out of three properties of your app, not of
your taste: how many top-level destinations there are, how deeply they nest, and
what the platform expects.

## The three chromes

### Bottom tabs

A fixed row of three to five destinations at the bottom of the screen, each flat
and equal. Wins on mobile, where the bottom edge is the reachable zone and the
small count keeps every destination one tap away. The hard ceiling is real: past
five tabs they shrink to unusable, and a "More" tab is a confession that the
model doesn't fit.

### Sidebar

A vertical list down the left edge, the only chrome that holds a **hierarchy**:
sections, groups, nesting, collapsible trees. Wins on desktop dashboards and
tools with many destinations. It trades horizontal room for that capacity, so it
collapses to icons or slides away when content needs the width. This is the rail
in the
<a href="/docs/app-screens/layouts/nav-rail-triptych">nav-rail + triptych</a>.

### Top nav

A horizontal bar across the top, usually with a logo and a handful of links.
Wins for **shallow** apps and marketing or content sites: few destinations, no
depth, a familiar web convention. It doesn't scale to many items (they overflow
or hide behind a hamburger) and can't express hierarchy on its own.

## Deciding axes

Read your app against three properties and the answer is usually forced.

| If…                                  | Lean toward    | Because                                          |
| ------------------------------------ | -------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| 3–5 flat destinations, mobile-first  | **Bottom tabs**| Thumb-reachable, every destination one tap away  |
| Many destinations, or any nesting    | **Sidebar**    | The only chrome that holds hierarchy             |
| Few destinations, shallow, web/content | **Top nav**  | Familiar, light, no depth to express             |
| Deep tool that's also used on phones | **Sidebar + tabs** | Sidebar on desktop, a tab subset on mobile  |

<DoDont>
  <Do title="Match the count to the chrome">
    Keep bottom tabs to five flat destinations. If the model is deeper, switch to
    a sidebar that can group and nest, rather than forcing depth into a bar that
    can't show it.
  </Do>
  <Dont title="Overload the chrome you picked">
    Eleven tabs hiding a tree behind a "More" sheet, or a top nav with three
    levels of dropdowns. When the navigation needs more than the chrome can show,
    the chrome is wrong, not too small.
  </Dont>
</DoDont>

## Combinations

Real apps mix these by scale rather than choosing one forever.

- **Sidebar + top bar.** Sidebar for primary destinations, a top bar for search,
  account, and global actions. The default for dense dashboards.
- **Responsive swap.** The same app shows a sidebar on desktop and a bottom tab
  bar (a subset of the same destinations) on mobile. Keep the destination set
  consistent so muscle memory survives the resize.
- **Tabs + stack.** Bottom tabs for top-level sections, a push/stack navigator
  inside each tab for depth. The standard mobile shape.

## Web vs. native expectations

The platform sets the default, and fighting it costs familiarity.

<PlatformTabs>
  <Platform label="Web">
    Top nav and sidebars are the native idioms; users expect global links up top
    and a left sidebar in dashboards. Each destination should be a real URL so it
    deep-links and the back button works. A bottom tab bar on the desktop web
    reads as a mobile port.
  </Platform>
  <Platform label="React Native">
    The bottom tab bar is the platform-native primary nav on phones (React
    Navigation's tab navigator, Expo Router tab groups); a left sidebar feels
    foreign on a handset. Tablets can promote to a sidebar or split view. Tabs
    typically each own a stack navigator, so depth lives inside a tab rather than
    in the bar.
  </Platform>
</PlatformTabs>

<Callout variant="tip" title="This decision, in one line">
  Count the top-level destinations and check whether they nest: 3–5 and flat
  wants tabs, many or nested wants a sidebar, few and shallow wants a top nav, and
  the platform breaks any tie.
</Callout>
