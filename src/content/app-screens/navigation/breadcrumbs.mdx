Breadcrumbs are a wayfinding aid: a trail from the root down to the current page
that answers two questions at once, **where am I in the hierarchy** and **how do
I move up a level**. They earn their space in deep, nested structures and become
pure noise in shallow ones.

<BreadcrumbsDemo />

## When you need them

- The hierarchy is **deep**: roughly three levels or more, where "up" isn't
  obvious from the page alone.
- Users **land mid-tree** from search, a deep link, or a notification, with no
  memory of the path they'd have clicked to get there.
- The structure is genuinely **hierarchical** (a file tree, nested categories,
  org → team → project), not a flat set of destinations.

And when you don't: flat apps, two-level structures, or anywhere a back action
and a clear page title already answer "where am I." On a phone, a full trail
rarely fits; show just the parent ("‹ Projects") instead.

## They show position, not history

The most common breadcrumb bug is treating them like a back button. Breadcrumbs
reflect the **hierarchy** (the fixed path from root to here), not the **history**
(the route you happened to take). If you arrived at a project from a search
results page, the breadcrumb still reads `Home › Projects › This project`, never
`Home › Search › This project`. Position is stable; history is per-visit. Use the
browser back button for history and breadcrumbs for structure.

<DoDont>
  <Do title="Mirror the hierarchy; current page isn't a link">
    Build the trail from the route's place in the tree, each ancestor linking up a
    level, and render the last crumb as plain text marked `aria-current="page"` —
    it's where you already are.
  </Do>
  <Dont title="Mirror the back stack or relink the current page">
    A trail that reflects how the user got here, or whose last crumb links to the
    page you're on, confuses position with history and adds a link that goes
    nowhere.
  </Dont>
</DoDont>

## Truncation

Long trails and small screens force collapse. Keep the two ends, the root and the
current page (plus usually its parent), and collapse the middle behind an `…` that
expands on click, as in the demo above. The ends are what orient; the middle is
what folds.

<Callout variant="tip" title="This page, in one line">
  Breadcrumbs show your fixed position in a deep hierarchy and a way up, not the
  history of how you arrived; use them past about three levels, make the last
  crumb plain text, and collapse the middle when space is tight.
</Callout>

They lean on the same routing that makes a view addressable, so the path is
derived from the URL, not from navigation history (see
<a href="/docs/app-screens/navigation/url-as-state">URL as state</a>), and they
complement rather than replace the primary nav chrome from the
<a href="/docs/app-screens/navigation/nav-pattern-picker">nav pattern picker</a>.
